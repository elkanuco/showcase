# Cash Flow Explained

## What Is Cash Flow?

Cash flow is the movement of money **into** and **out of** an organization over a period of time.

In simple terms:

> Cash flow shows where money comes from and where money goes.

```text
Cash Inflows  -  Cash Outflows  =  Net Cash Flow
```

If inflows are greater than outflows:

```text
Positive Cash Flow
```

If outflows are greater than inflows:

```text
Negative Cash Flow
```

---

# Simple Example

Imagine you have:

```text
Salary received        + €3,000
Rent paid              - €1,000
Food                   - €500
Utilities              - €200
--------------------------------
Net Cash Flow          + €1,300
```

You generated a positive cash flow of **€1,300**.

---

# Types of Cash Flows

## Cash Inflows

Money received by an organization.

Examples:

- Loan repayments received
- Interest income
- Bond issuance proceeds
- Investment income
- Fees and commissions

```text
Cash coming in
```

---

## Cash Outflows

Money paid by an organization.

Examples:

- Project loan disbursements
- Debt repayments
- Salaries
- Operating expenses
- Interest payments

```text
Cash going out
```

---

# Cash Flow at the EIB

For the European Investment Bank (EIB), cash flows are much larger and more complex.

## Major Cash Inflows

### Bond Issuance

The EIB raises money from investors.

```text
Investors
     ↓
 Buy EIB Bonds
     ↓
Cash received by EIB
```

Example:

```text
EIB issues €5 billion bond
↓
EIB receives €5 billion cash
```

---

### Loan Repayments

Borrowers repay:

- Principal
- Interest

```text
Borrower
    ↓
Repayment
    ↓
EIB receives cash
```

---

### Investment Income

Treasury investments generate:

- Interest income
- Investment returns

---

# Major Cash Outflows

## Project Financing

The EIB's primary business is lending.

```text
EIB
   ↓
Disburses loan
   ↓
Project receives funds
```

Examples:

- Renewable energy projects
- Railway projects
- Infrastructure projects
- Innovation financing

---

## Bond Repayments

When bonds mature:

```text
EIB
   ↓
Repays investors
```

This can involve billions of euros.

---

## Operating Expenses

Examples include:

- Staff costs
- IT systems
- Buildings
- Consulting services

---

# Cash Flow Lifecycle at the EIB

A simplified example:

```text
Step 1
EIB issues bonds
+ €10 billion

Step 2
EIB lends money to projects
- €7 billion

Step 3
Projects repay loans
+ €2 billion

Step 4
EIB repays maturing debt
- €3 billion

Net Cash Position:
+ €2 billion
```

Treasury continuously manages these movements.

# Why Cash Flow Is Important

## 1. Ability to Pay Obligations

The bank must always have cash available for:

- Debt repayments
- Supplier payments
- Employee salaries
- Project disbursements

Without sufficient cash flow, operations may be disrupted.

---

## 2. Liquidity Management

Cash flow directly affects liquidity.

```text
Good Cash Flow
         ↓
Strong Liquidity
         ↓
Ability to meet obligations
```

Treasury teams forecast future cash inflows and outflows to ensure enough liquidity is available.

---

## 3. Planning and Forecasting

Large banks forecast cash flows over:

- Days
- Weeks
- Months
- Years

This helps determine:

- When to issue bonds
- When funding is needed
- How much cash reserve to maintain

---

## 4. Risk Management

Unexpected cash outflows can create liquidity risk.

Examples:

```text
Large bond maturity
Unexpected market stress
Higher loan disbursements
```

Cash flow forecasting helps identify risks before they occur.

---

# Cash Flow vs Profit

These concepts are different.

## Profit

```text
Revenue - Expenses
```

Measured according to accounting rules.

---

## Cash Flow

```text
Actual cash received
minus
Actual cash paid
```

Measured using real money movements.

---

Example:

```text
Loan approved today
Loan repayment due next year
```

The bank may record income today but not receive cash until later.

Therefore:

```text
Profitable ≠ Cash Rich
```

An institution can be profitable on paper but still face cash shortages.

# Cash Flow Statement

Organizations produce a Cash Flow Statement showing:

## Operating Cash Flows

Generated from normal business activities.

Examples:

- Interest received
- Interest paid
- Operating expenses

---

## Investing Cash Flows

Related to investments and assets.

Examples:

- Investment purchases
- Investment sales

---

## Financing Cash Flows

Related to borrowing and funding.

Examples:

- Bond issuance
- Debt repayment
- Capital injections

---

# Treasury Perspective

For a Treasury department, cash flow forecasting answers questions such as:

```text
How much cash do we have today?

How much cash will we need next week?

How much debt matures this month?

When should we issue new bonds?

How much liquidity reserve is required?
```

These forecasts are critical for maintaining financial stability.

---

# Key Takeaway

> Cash flow is the movement of cash into and out of an organization. At the EIB or any investment bank, cash flow management ensures that sufficient funds are available to finance projects, repay debt, maintain liquidity, support daily operations, and preserve market confidence.
